PROFESSOR FEEDBACK POINT 5: "Reframe the contribution and novelty"
================================================================================

Scope: reversible token mapping was framed as a differentiator in
isolation (against OpenAI's Privacy Filter in the abstract, and implicitly
against "rule-based detectors" in general in the Introduction). Confirmed
during the earlier professor-response pass that this doesn't hold:
Presidio supports reversible anonymisation through its own encrypt/decrypt
operator pair, so reversibility per se is not this thesis's own property.
Reworded both instances to move the contribution claim from "reversibility"
in isolation to the end-to-end combination (local detection, reversible
typed tokens, external semantic routing, deterministic server-side
execution), per the professor's own suggested framing, and only where that
framing is grounded in the systematic comparison background.tex already
conducts, not asserted for free.

Files touched: Impaginazione/inizio.tex, capitoli/Introduction.tex.
File untouched: capitoli/background.tex — already states the Presidio
reversible-operator fact correctly (see "already correct" note below).
Files untouched: capitoli/architecture.tex, capitoli/Implementation.tex —
neither makes a reversibility-as-novelty or exclusivity claim; both
already describe GLiNER and Presidio as interchangeable inputs to the same
token/reversibility mechanism, consistent with the corrected framing.

FILE: Impaginazione/inizio.tex
CHAPTER/SECTION: Abstract
================================================================================

--- OLD ---
The approach draws on the broader state of the art in privacy-preserving
LLM infrastructure. In particular, OpenAI's Privacy Filter (Apache 2.0,
2025), recently made open source, confirms that lightweight, on-premise
NER models incorporated as pre-processing layers represent the emerging
industry standard for PII containment. The key architectural contribution
of this thesis relative to that reference model is \emph{reversibility}:
whilst one-way encoding destroys the mapping between placeholders and
entities, the token-based scheme proposed here preserves full operational
correctness whilst allowing controlled de-anonymisation at the protocol
boundary, a distinction relevant under Article 4.5 of the GDPR, which
classifies such processing as pseudonymisation rather than anonymisation.

--- NEW ---
The approach draws on the broader state of the art in privacy-preserving
LLM infrastructure. OpenAI's Privacy Filter (Apache 2.0, 2025), recently
made open source, confirms that lightweight, on-premise NER models
incorporated as pre-processing layers represent an emerging pattern for
PII containment; it performs one-way redaction, however, discarding the
mapping between placeholders and entities once masking is complete.
Reversibility on its own is not unique to this work, Presidio supports it
too, through a dedicated encrypt/decrypt operator pair. What this thesis
contributes relative to that broader landscape is the combination: local
detection, reversible typed tokens, external semantic routing, and
deterministic server-side execution operating together end to end,
preserving full operational correctness whilst allowing controlled
de-anonymisation at the protocol boundary, a distinction relevant under
Article 4.5 of the GDPR, which classifies such processing as
pseudonymisation rather than anonymisation.

--- WHY ---
"The key architectural contribution... is reversibility" asserted
reversibility itself as the differentiator without acknowledging Presidio
also has it, so the claim didn't hold once checked. Kept the true part of
the Privacy Filter comparison (it genuinely is one-way and discards the
mapping), added the Presidio acknowledgment explicitly, and moved the
actual contribution claim to the four-part combination. Also softened
"the emerging industry standard" to "an emerging pattern" — one model
release doesn't establish an industry standard.

FILE: capitoli/Introduction.tex
CHAPTER/SECTION: Section 1.4, "Contributions" (opening sentence)
================================================================================

--- OLD ---
This thesis makes three concrete contributions, positioned against the
state of the art reviewed in Chapter~\ref{ch:background}: rule-based
detectors and one-way redaction models each solve part of the
PII-exposure problem, but neither preserves a path back to the real data
once a routing decision has been made.

--- NEW ---
This thesis makes three concrete contributions, positioned against the
state of the art reviewed in Chapter~\ref{ch:background}. Individually,
rule-based PII detection, reversible token mapping, and LLM-based
semantic routing are each established techniques on their own;
Section~\ref{sec:background_sota} finds no reviewed system that combines
local detection, reversible typed tokens, external semantic routing, and
deterministic server-side execution end to end.

--- WHY ---
This was not just an overclaim but a factual error: "neither [rule-based
detectors nor one-way redaction models] preserves a path back to the real
data" is false for rule-based detectors specifically, since Presidio
does preserve one via its operator pair. Replaced with the claim
background.tex's own SOTA section (Section~\ref{sec:background_sota})
actually earned through its five-system comparison (Presidio, GLiNER,
Privacy Filter, Hou et al. 2025, Albanese et al. 2026): no reviewed system
combines all four elements end to end. This is the professor's explicit
instruction followed literally — "avoid claiming that no prior system
provides this combination unless that conclusion follows from a more
systematic review of the literature" — the claim now rests on the review
that was actually done, not asserted independently of it.
